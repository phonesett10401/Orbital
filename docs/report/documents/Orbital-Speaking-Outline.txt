— a script written by somebody else gets read aloud, and being read aloud to
is worse than being talked to.

                  leaving roughly three minutes for questions

next.

SLIDE 2 of 13   ·   1:00   ·   Han Phyo
The Problem: Fragmented & Closed
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ON SCREEN   Four cards: Fragmented, Raw, Metered, Closed.
THE POINT   The data already exists. Nothing assembles it.

SAY         · Every one of these three object types is tracked publicly, right
              now, by somebody.
            · But each lives behind its own API, in its own format, with its
              own rate limit — and some forbid the use outright.
            · Nobody has to invent the data. The work is in assembling it and
              keeping it free to look at.
            · Do not read all four cards. Pick two and let the slide carry the
              rest.

HAND OVER   "So the shape of the system follows from that."

SLIDE 3 of 13   ·   1:00   ·   Bhone
Architecture & Methodology
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ON SCREEN   Three stacked arrows: Ingestion, API, Frontend.
THE POINT   One direction of flow, and each layer knows only the one beneath
            it.

SAY         · Data flows one way. Ingestion talks to the outside world; the
              API reads what ingestion left; the frontend reads the API.
            · The frontend never learns where the data came from, and
              ingestion never learns a browser exists.
            · One shared data shape covers all three object types, which is
              why a new type costs one module.
            · This is the slide the later architecture slides build on — set
              it up properly here and slide 9 goes quickly.

HAND OVER   "The question then is whether it is actually feasible."

SLIDE 4 of 13   ·   1:10   ·   Nyan Lin
Feasibility & Performance
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ON SCREEN   Bar chart: four backend stages at 2,000, 10,000 and 30,000
            objects.
THE POINT   Free data is viable; the constraint is rate limiting, not
            availability.

SAY         · Every source is free and public. Total cash cost is zero — that
              is in the report as a table.
            · The real budget is request quota, and it was budgeted like
              money: 77% of the OpenSky allowance planned, and the app refuses
              to start above 85%.
            · On the chart: the left-hand group is where the system actually
              runs. Every stage under two milliseconds.
            · The 43.5 ms bar on the right is the cliff the 2,000 cap exists
              to avoid — the backend is one event loop, so a slow call delays
              everyone.

HAND OVER   "And when a source fails anyway — which one did."

SLIDE 5 of 13   ·   1:10   ·   Han Phyo
Reliability & Use case
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ON SCREEN   Two cards, and the use case diagram: eleven cases, five actors.
THE POINT   Upstream failure is expected, not exceptional.

SAY         · Treat a feed going dark as normal operating conditions, not as
              an incident.
            · Stale-but-flagged: the last good data keeps being served, with
              its age visible. The map never goes blank.
            · Aircraft and ships each have two independent feeds, so one
              failure is never fatal.
            · On the diagram, point out that four of the eleven cases have no
              human actor — they are the scheduler, and that is where every
              external dependency lives.

HAND OVER   "That is the system. Here is how the work was planned."

The plan, and the three milestones
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ON SCREEN   Gantt chart, twelve weeks, three milestone markers.
THE POINT   This is the plan, not a chart redrawn afterwards to match.

SAY         · Three milestones: requirements signed off at week 3, feature

            · The critical path is the first vertical slice — requirements,
              data contract, one provider, the store, the API, one layer on
              screen.
            · After that it is parallel. Satellites, ships and accounts each

NOTE        Milestones at weeks 3, 8 and 11. The critical path is the first
            vertical slice; everything after it runs in parallel because the
            shared data shape was fixed before any provider was written.

SLIDE 8 of 13   ·   1:10   ·   Pyae
Risks, and what they cost
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ON SCREEN   Four risk cards. R1 is marked HAPPENED.
THE POINT   One of these actually happened, and it cost what it was designed
            to.

SAY         · R1 is not hypothetical. OpenSky became unreachable from every
              cloud host, mid-project.
            · It cost one provider module and one registry entry. Nothing
              above the ingestion layer changed — that is the architecture
              being proved by an accident rather than by an argument.
            · R4 is worth a number: the client polls every ten seconds but the
              data only changes every three hundred, so twenty-nine polls in
              thirty are answered by a header comparison that never builds a
              response.
            · If asked why these four out of ten: these are the four with a
              structural mitigation — something in the architecture exists
              because of them.

NOTE        R1 is the one that actually happened: the architecture was
            designed so that losing a feed costs one module, and when OpenSky
            went it cost exactly that. R4 is the one with a ceiling, and be
            ready for the follow-up - scaling is vertical only, because a
            second worker would open a second AIS socket and the two stores
            would disagree. Past a bigger box the design has to change, and
            4.6 says so. The full register of ten is section 4.6.

HAND OVER   "So, the design itself. Three layers."

SLIDE 9 of 13   ·   1:00   ·   Bhone
Three layers, one direction
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ON SCREEN   Full architecture diagram: five feeds, three layers.
THE POINT   The browser never talks to a data source.

SAY         · Every external call goes through the backend, so rate limiting
              is enforced in exactly one place.
            · A hundred open tabs cost the same upstream quota as one. That is
              the single most important property on this slide.
            · It is also why adding a second aircraft feed was a backend-only
              change: the frontend was never told, because there was nothing
              to tell it.
            · Upstream failure is contained at layer one — the API serves the
              last good snapshot with a staleness flag.

NOTE        Five external feeds, three internal layers. The single most
            important property: upstream failure is contained at layer 1.

HAND OVER   "Underneath all of that, the database is two tables."

SLIDE 10 of 13   ·   0:55   ·   Bhone
Two tables — and that is the design
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ON SCREEN   ER diagram: two persisted tables, the rest dashed.
THE POINT   A tracker looks like it needs a big database. This one
            deliberately does not.

SAY         · Accounts and sessions. That is the whole persisted schema.
            · Everything the map shows lives in memory and is replaced every
              poll. Nothing about an aircraft is ever written to disk.
            · So there is no database on the request path, and no schema
              migration for the thing that changes most often — the shape of a
              feed.
            · Be honest about the cost: Orbital cannot answer a question about
              last Tuesday. The entitlement windows are bounded by what is
              still in memory.

NOTE        The honest cost: Orbital cannot answer a question about last
            Tuesday. Entitlement windows are bounded by what is still in
            memory, not by a query.

HAND OVER   "One class explains why the second feed was cheap."

SLIDE 11 of 13   ·   0:55   ·   Bhone
Why a second feed cost one file
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ON SCREEN   Class diagram: Provider, seven implementations, UnionProvider
            highlighted.
THE POINT   UnionProvider is a Provider and holds two Providers.

SAY         · One abstract Provider: fetch, given a bounding box, return the
              normalised shape.
            · UnionProvider inherits it and holds two of them — primary every
              poll, supplement every 120 seconds.
            · A caller cannot tell a pair of feeds from a single feed. That is
              the whole trick, and it is why losing a primary source changed
              nothing above ingestion.
            · Worth adding: the API never holds a Provider. There is no call
              path from a request to a socket, which is why an upstream
              failure cannot reach a response.

NOTE        Seven concrete providers, one abstraction. The API never holds a
            Provider, so there is no call path from a request to a socket.

HAND OVER   "And this is what it all looks like to a user."

BEFORE THE RUN-THROUGH
==============================================================================

  · Agree the split above, then time yourselves individually. Most
    people run 20% long on the first attempt.
  · Slides 4 and 8 both touch the object-count limit. Whoever speaks
    second should point back rather than repeat the numbers.
  · Decide who takes questions on what, so nobody answers over anyone.
    The obvious ones: why free sources, why one worker, what happens
    when a feed dies, and could it be commercial.
  · Have the live site open in a tab. If the demonstration is asked
    for, orbital-liveview.vercel.app — and the landing page is at
    /landing.
